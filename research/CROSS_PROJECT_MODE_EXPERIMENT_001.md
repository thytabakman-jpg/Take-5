## External-comparator frame

Relevant existing research gives four comparator families:

1. Independent judgment before interaction.
Social-influence work shows early information sharing can reduce diversity, while independent judgments followed by structured discussion can improve accuracy under some conditions.

2. Deliberation/aggregation.
Small-group deliberation can outperform raw aggregation in some tasks, but benefits depend on reasoning structure and calibration.

3. Cognitive forcing/debiasing.
Explicit debiasing strategies are not uniformly effective and can induce over-search or fail to reduce error.

4. LLM multi-path / multi-agent reasoning.
Tree-style exploration, debate, and diverse reasoning can improve some tasks, but recent comparisons show multi-agent discussion is not universally superior and is sensitive to diversity, interaction protocol, and task structure.

Our distinctive hypothesis is not "independence always wins."
It is mode-sensitive ordering:
independent expansion where premature pruning is the risk,
focused contraction where exact discrimination is the job,
then delegated action when intervention is licensed.
