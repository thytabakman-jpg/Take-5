## Core correction

A question is not identical to a missing fact or a gap.

Relative to a live information context s, use the project model:

q = <P,I>_s

P
the question's presuppositional/background commitments.

I
the issue: the family of information states that count as resolving the question.

For the current model:

I is nonempty.
I is downward closed.
The members of I cover the live context s.
A later information state t resolves q exactly when t is in I.
The question is nontrivial in s when s itself is not already in I.

This is a project model informed by formal question semantics, not a claim that every theory
of questions has this exact ontology.

## What happened to "structured gap"

Retained as an intuition for unresolvedness.

The gap is state-relative:

current information does not yet satisfy the question's resolution condition.

The gap is not itself the question.

## Grade 5–6 projection

A question carries three things:

ASSUMES
What does the wording already take for granted?

OPENS
What possibilities is it asking us to distinguish?

SETTLES
What would count as enough to answer it?

Student shorthand:

QUESTION = ASSUMES + OPENS + SETTLES

## Sukkos binding

Primary Sukkos-native bridge:

Kohelet 7:10.

The verse tells the reader not to ask why earlier days were better than the present ones,
and explicitly says that this is not a wise question.

Rema, Orach Chayim 490:9 records the custom of reading Kohelet on Sukkot.

Educational application:

The question "Why were the earlier days better?" places "the earlier days were better"
inside the frame before the answer begins.

The booklet may ask learners to notice that hidden commitment, reopen the possibilities,
and repair the question.

Source boundary:

Kohelet 7:10 says the question is not asked from wisdom.
The formal diagnosis in terms of presupposition/frame is the project's educational analysis,
not a claim that the verse itself states modern formal semantics.

## Mechanism

Encounter a question that looks normal.
Detect what it takes for granted.
Separate the assumption from what is genuinely open.
Rewrite the question so the answer is not forced by the wording.
Specify what would settle it.

## Route skeleton

question-looking sentence

-> Sukkos reveal through Kohelet 7:10

## Observable evidence

On a new question, the learner can:

identify one built-in assumption,
state at least two genuinely live alternatives when appropriate,
say what evidence would settle the issue,
and rewrite the question so the disputed assumption is no longer smuggled in.

## Alternatives considered

A1
Structured gap:
useful intuition but insufficient as identity because unresolvedness is a state, not the full question object.

A2
Issue/resolution plus physical sukkah sufficiency:
formally strong but the holiday relation risks becoming a classification example rather than a direct question text.

A3
Frame/resolution plus Kohelet:
preserves formal resolution structure, adds presupposition, supplies a Sukkos-native text
that explicitly judges the wisdom of a question, and yields an independent repair operation.

A3.
