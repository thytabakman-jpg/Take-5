Question: with hundreds of jobs/tools and newly available MTA-style mathematical reduction, what belongs at the architectural center without either bloating the center with every tool or leaving it epistemically empty?

## Finding 1 — Two-layer model is insufficient

"tiny kernel law + hundreds of tools" leaves a missing architectural responsibility.

The law can constrain authoritative transitions, but it does not itself determine:
- what kind of problem is present;
- which obligations are live;
- which analysis family is relevant;
- when a newly discovered object changes the problem representation;
- which tools should interact;
- when evidence is sufficient to propose an authoritative transition.

Putting all of that into the kernel bloats it.
Leaving all of it to arbitrary tools creates incoherent routing.

Therefore a middle abstraction is justified.

## Finding 2 — Candidate three-scale architecture

L0 GOVERNANCE INVARIANTS
Small, globally protected laws:
- typed authoritative transition;
- evidence != authority;
- unresolved != PASS;
- effect-scoped admission/authority;
- no self-promotion;
- provenance/currentness sufficient for legal continuation;
- self-modification governed.

L1 EPISTEMIC/CONTROL ALGEBRA
Small reusable mathematical jobs that organize arbitrary tools:
- DIFFERENTIATE
- RELATE
- RECONSTRUCT
- STRENGTHEN
plus:
- representation/frame expansion;
- interaction residual detection;
- admission/evaluation;
- routing/reentry;
- consequence/closure propagation.

These are not necessarily all primitive. They are candidate generators/organizers of tool behavior.

L2 TOOL ECOLOGY
Hundreds/unbounded concrete tools, composites, specialist profiles, project-local methods and future generated tools.

Architecture should make L2 replaceable while preserving L0 and a compact L1 interface.

## Finding 3 — "audit all tools" has a precise job

Do not audit all tools to decide which named tools enter the kernel.

Audit them to recover the quotient/generating basis of their protected behavior.

Let Tool set be T.

Define job-relative behavioral equivalence:

t_i ~_J t_j
iff
they are mutually reconstructible for protected job effects under allowed composition/basis.

Then seek a small generating basis B* such that:

ProtectedBehavior(T) subseteq Cl(B*)

while preserving irreducible residuals and incomparable behavior.

This is exactly where MTA + Multi-Object + PD + architecture analysis belong.

The target is not "best tools."
The target is the smallest high-ceiling behavioral algebra that can reconstruct the tool ecology.

## Finding 6 — Architecture Analysis itself

Architecture Analysis does not belong wholesale at L0.

Its irreducible mathematical contribution appears to be:
given a protected job and candidate system, recover typed roles, interfaces, authority, state, transitions, dependencies and preservation obligations, then test whether responsibilities are misplaced, duplicated, missing or bypassable.

This may factor largely into:
DIFFERENTIATE + RELATE + RECONSTRUCT + representation/interaction challenge.

Therefore Architecture Analysis is a prime object for MTA reduction during the tool-basis audit.

## Finding 7 — Proposed discovery program

Do not blindly run every tool against every tool.

Use a quotient-and-residual audit:

1 Recover protected behavior for every tool/family.
2 MTA each high-leverage family to expose mathematical jobs.
3 Cluster by job-relative equivalence/reconstructibility.
4 Multi-Object on clusters to find irreducible interaction residuals.
5 PD/PDAudit on hidden frames/selectors.
6 derive candidate generating basis.
7 reconstruct every protected behavior from basis + allowed composition.
8 preserve residuals that fail reconstruction as additional candidates.
9 architecture analysis places surviving factors at L0/L1/L2.
10 RTC/MTOS attacks the basis for smaller/higher-ceiling successors.
11 validate against historical and unfamiliar holdouts.
