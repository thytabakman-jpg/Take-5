Canonical quantity: Mutual information.

I(X;Y) = H(X) - H(X|Y)

Interpretation used here: how much observing Y reduces uncertainty about target X, on average.

Page-1 educational move: compare two clues/questions relative to the same target. One can strongly change what we know about X; another can tell us little or nothing about X.

Forbidden: "mutual information is the equation for a question."

Bridge: a useful question is one whose answer is informative about the target.

Boundary: literal mutual-information values require a joint probability model. Classroom examples can illustrate the relation without claiming numeric MI for unsupplied everyday distributions.
