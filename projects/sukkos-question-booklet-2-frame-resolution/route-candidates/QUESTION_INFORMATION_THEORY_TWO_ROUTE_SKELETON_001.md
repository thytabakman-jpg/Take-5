## Shared four-page architecture

Whole movement:

IS / REALITY
-> HUMAN TECHNOLOGY
-> SUKKOS / RELIGIOUS TECHNOLOGY ADDS SOMETHING
-> AGENCY / TRANSFER

Protected page jobs:

1. Page 1 establishes a real phenomenon or formal structure.
2. Page 2 gives the learner a usable human technology in response to that reality.
3. Page 3 shows what Sukkos/Jewish practice adds to, embodies, deepens, or constrains that technology. It may not be a decorative analogy.
4. Page 4 requires independent transfer.

Each page ends with a question that creates the next page's job.

Shared learner context:
- Grade 5-6.
- One student especially struggles to generate questions despite understanding questions intellectually.
- Question construction anatomy remains available as a Page-2 scaffold:
  question word + auxiliary verb + subject + main verb.
- The booklet remains a Sukkos education booklet; learner-support needs are subordinate to the holiday-booklet architecture.

Shared Sukkos source candidate:
Vayikra Rabbah 30:12 maps the four species across two distinctions:
- etrog: taste + smell
- lulav/date palm: taste + no smell
- hadas/myrtle: no taste + smell
- aravah/willow: no taste + no smell
and then instructs that all be bound together as one grouping.
This gives a genuine Sukkos-native distinction structure and a religious move from classification to integration.

# VERSION 1 — SHANNON ENTROPY ROUTE

## Overview

Central mathematics:

H(X) = - sum_x p(x) log_2 p(x)

Core idea:
uncertainty can be measured.

Booklet thesis:
Questions are tools for reducing a space of possibilities. A person can use a question to make uncertainty smaller, and Sukkos supplies a ritual/textual case where distinctions identify different types while the ritual still binds all types together.

### Page 1 — IS / REALITY

Job:
Show that uncertainty is a real measurable structure, not just a feeling.

What the page is doing:
Introduce Shannon entropy as a real mathematical quantity for uncertainty. Use a simple finite possibility set so students can see that more live possibilities means more uncertainty.

Learner takeaway:
There can be a measurable amount of "not knowing."

End question:
"If uncertainty can be measured, what can we do to make it smaller?"

### Page 2 — HUMAN TECHNOLOGY

Job:
Show that questions are a human technology for reducing uncertainty, then give the learner a construction scaffold for making one.

What the page is doing:
Use a simple mystery/possibility set. A useful yes/no question divides the live possibilities. Then move from the function of a question to how a learner can build one:
question word + auxiliary verb + subject + main verb.

Learner takeaway:
I do not need to invent a question all at once. I can build a question that helps reduce what I do not know.

End question:
"Can Sukkos show us what happens when distinctions help us tell different possibilities apart?"

### Page 3 — SUKKOS / RELIGIOUS TECHNOLOGY

Job:
Show a Sukkos-native system of distinctions and then show what the religious layer adds beyond classification.

What the page is doing:
Use Vayikra Rabbah 30:12. Taste and smell create four combinations that distinguish the four species. The Midrash then makes a second move: the four differentiated types are bound together as one grouping.

Learner takeaway:
Sukkos does not erase distinctions. It uses distinctions to see what is different, then ritualizes holding the different parts together.

Religious surplus:
The human technology says "distinguish to reduce uncertainty."
The Sukkos technology adds "distinguish accurately without turning difference into separation."

End question:
"Can I use questions to tell possibilities apart and still keep sight of the whole?"

### Page 4 — AGENCY / TRANSFER

Job:
Have the learner independently use the technology.

What the page is doing:
Give a new case with multiple live possibilities. The learner identifies what they do not know, builds one or more questions, and uses the answers to reduce the possibility space. The task can include a final whole-system view so classification does not become exclusion.

Learner takeaway:
When I do not know, I can build a question that reduces uncertainty.

Closing question:
"What do I not know yet, and what question can I build to make the possibilities smaller?"

# VERSION 2 — MUTUAL INFORMATION ROUTE

## Overview

Central mathematics:

I(X;Y) = H(X) - H(X|Y)

Core idea:
information is target-relative; learning one thing is useful when it reduces uncertainty about the thing you actually care about.

Booklet thesis:
Not every question is equally useful. A useful question asks about something whose answer tells you about your target. Sukkos supplies a native classification case in which some distinctions reveal the species and others do not, while the Midrash then transforms classification into communal integration.

### Page 1 — IS / REALITY

Job:
Show that information has a relationship structure: one variable can tell us something about another.

What the page is doing:
Introduce mutual information through a simple target and clue. Compare a clue that changes what we know about the target with a clue that does not.

Learner takeaway:
Some information tells me a lot about what I am trying to find out. Some tells me almost nothing.

End question:
"What makes one question more useful than another?"

### Page 2 — HUMAN TECHNOLOGY

Job:
Turn target-relative information into a question-making procedure.

What the page is doing:
Teach a human strategy:
1. decide what I want to know;
2. identify a distinction whose answer would tell me something about that target;
3. build the question.
Use the four-part question anatomy as the construction scaffold:
question word + auxiliary verb + subject + main verb.

Learner takeaway:
A useful question is not just grammatically complete. Its answer has to tell me something about my target.

End question:
"Can Sukkos show us a case where the right distinctions reveal what we are looking at?"

### Page 3 — SUKKOS / RELIGIOUS TECHNOLOGY

Job:
Give a Sukkos-native case where the usefulness of a distinction depends on the target, then show the religious surplus.

What the page is doing:
Use Vayikra Rabbah 30:12. If the target is "Which of the four species is this?", taste and smell are informative distinctions in the Midrash's classification. An irrelevant feature would not identify the category. The Midrash then refuses to stop at sorting; all four are bound together.

Learner takeaway:
The right question depends on what I am trying to learn. Sukkos also teaches that identifying differences is not the same as dividing the group.

Religious surplus:
The human technology says "choose distinctions that inform the target."
The Sukkos technology adds "use distinctions to understand parts while still binding the differentiated parts into one whole."

End question:
"Can I choose and build a question whose answer actually tells me what I need to know?"

### Page 4 — AGENCY / TRANSFER

Job:
Have the learner independently choose and construct an informative question.

What the page is doing:
Give a new target plus several possible questions or question components. The learner identifies the target, rejects irrelevant distinctions, constructs a useful question, and explains how its answer would change what they know.

Learner takeaway:
When I want to know something, I can ask: "What answer would actually give me information about my target?" Then I can build that question.

Closing question:
"What am I trying to find out, and what question would actually give me information about it?"
