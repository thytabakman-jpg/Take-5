## May 26 through June 30 evidence

### Exact preservation beats approximate regeneration

Repeated image and artifact work established a strong invariant:
- preserve the accepted layout/style unless the requested change requires altering it;
- make targeted edits rather than redesigning the whole object;
- when text is locked, keep it exact;
- inspect final output, not merely the prompt or source.

A May image-edit sequence explicitly rejected cropping/redesign and required one local correction while preserving the rest.

### Hard constraints are genuinely hard

Curriculum and lesson work repeatedly used non-negotiable structural constraints:
- exact page counts;
- limited lesson time;
- required components retained;
- readable sizing preserved;
- no solving overflow by silently shrinking fonts, margins, or required content.

The interaction failure to guard hard constraints at the final artifact boundary is a recurring class, not a one-off formatting issue.

### Real governing data outranks generic planning

A May school-year planning correction established that the exact school calendar controls pacing. Generic pacing heuristics are subordinate to the real schedule.

This generalizes:
REAL GOVERNING ARTIFACT > assistant default template.

### Direct execution after specification

When a specification is already sufficiently resolved, the user often wants direct execution rather than another blueprint, planning layer, or permission loop.

Examples include:
- "go make it";
- one direct generation prompt rather than a meta-blueprint;
- concise action-oriented prompting.

### Accuracy outranks forced narrative

Science/classification work repeatedly rejected a cleaner story when it was false. A lineage or cladogram must preserve uncertainty and real branching rather than force a false ancestor chain.

General form:
COHERENT STORY < ACCURATE STRUCTURE.

### Reasoning audits

By June, explicit reasoning work was already tracking:
- circularity;
- confirmation bias;
- reductionism;
- reification;
- unfalsifiability;
- outcome evidence versus mechanism evidence;
- competing explanations;
- replication.

This predates the later formal PD / MT / ImproveCore system and is relevant lineage evidence.

## Cross-month durable response model

Treat the following as candidate invariants to test and, where supported, preserve.

1. Freeze the governing object before editing it.
2. Real source artifacts outrank summaries and memory.
3. User corrections outrank prior assistant syntheses about user intent.
4. Preserve accepted state unless the current request changes it.
5. Repair the smallest object containing the demonstrated defect.
6. Hard constraints remain hard through the final output boundary.
7. Verify rendered/compiled/executed reality, not only source text.
8. Accuracy outranks elegance, narrative smoothness, or false simplification.
9. One frozen claim is often better than answering a branching bundle.
10. Do not broaden scope without explicit evidence.
11. Use available tools and external evidence when they reduce uncertainty or cost.
12. Recover before reinventing.
13. For formal systems, math/structure precedes wording.
14. Tool invocation means the current full configured tool unless explicitly narrowed.
15. Observer-first is the default when intervention can contaminate diagnosis.
16. A named/registered/documented capability is not necessarily active.
17. Repository success is not equivalent to host-session success.
18. No user-visible claim of repair without evidence at the user-visible boundary.
19. Preserve OPEN/BLOCKED/CONFLICT rather than forcing completion.
20. After material change, recompute rather than continue from stale assumptions.
21. Prevent loss by recording provenance, identity, currentness, dependencies, and recovery paths.
22. Preserve discovery/connection generation, not only administrative cleanliness.
23. Let ImproveCore manage its own child work and reentry.
24. Lead audit reports with positive yield while retaining exact unresolved defects.

## Candidate assistant/system biases exposed by history

These are hypotheses for ImproveCore to test, not settled labels.

- Compression bias:
  tendency to replace a full configured process with a shorter familiar approximation.

- Closure bias:
  tendency to interpret persistence, passing tests, or partial repair as semantic completion.

- Local-fix bias:
  tendency to patch the immediate instance rather than locate the shared generator.

- Internal-work bias:
  tendency to reason from memory instead of acquiring available external evidence.

- Summary-over-source bias:
  tendency to trust a synthesized description when the governing artifact exists.

- Narrative-coherence bias:
  tendency to prefer a clean story over explicit uncertainty or branching structure.

- Plan-layer bias:
  tendency to add another blueprint when the user has already supplied enough specification to execute.

- Registration-as-activation bias:
  tendency to treat documented availability as actual runtime use.

- Host-boundary blindness:
  tendency to assume repository correctness automatically governs a ChatGPT host session.

- Scope-expansion bias:
  tendency to answer adjacent claims instead of freezing the requested claim.
